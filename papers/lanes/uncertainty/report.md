# Uncertainty, intervals and simulation precision: acquisition and reading report

Acquired material is 19 PDFs and one genuine PMC full HTML article, with reproducible URL/hash/time sidecars. The catalogue distinguishes an acquired document from a document read in depth. Most acquired English papers were inspected at the methods, assumptions, results and limitations relevant to this lane; the 105-page handbook chapter was read selectively. The Chinese originals were acquired and their English abstracts inspected, with deeper translated reading outstanding. The Atılgan PDF lacks a useful text layer; its author abstract was read through ERIC. This is a substantial primary-source map, not a claim to have read every page or exhausted every language, thesis repository and statistical inference extension.

## Four uncertainties that the library must distinguish

1. **Measurement uncertainty:** relative/absolute error variance, SEM/CSEM, and uncertainty about a person's universe score.
2. **Estimation uncertainty:** sampling variation of estimated variance components, error variances and reliability coefficients from a finite G-study dataset.
3. **Projected-design uncertainty:** the reliability of a proposed D-study when the input G-study components are estimated, rather than known parameters.
4. **Simulation uncertainty:** Monte Carlo variation in claims about a method's bias, standard error, interval coverage or failures.

A supplied-component D-study projection is deterministic conditional on those inputs. A SEM computed from them is not the standard error of the estimated SEM, and an interval around a person's score is not an interval around a reliability coefficient. This distinction should be visible in future APIs and documentation.

The current [Lee and Harris chapter](https://ncme.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Educational-Measurement-Fifth-Edition-Chapter-05.pdf), printed pp.360–361, emphasizes that reliability estimates themselves have error. Its caution about few levels sampled from infinite facets matters especially to GT: increasing persons does not manufacture additional raters, tasks or occasions. Resampling one administration can miss error attributable to genuine alternate forms, occasions and test-taker consistency.

[CASMA 58](https://education.uiowa.edu/sites/education.uiowa.edu/files/2026-04/casma-research-report-58-archived.pdf) provides valuable numerical comparisons and links formulas to the chapter. Its two half forms are deliberately balanced for content/statistics and constructed from a single session. They are an item-sampling replication benchmark with other contextual influences held constant. They cannot validate an all sources reliability claim or an uncertainty procedure merely because a computed coefficient matches the half-form correlation.

## Analytic interval lineage

The primary GT trail begins with Smith 1978 component stability, Smith 1981 multiple G-study designs, Smith 1982 variance-component confidence intervals, Bell 1986 simultaneous intervals, ACT 87-7, Brennan 2001 chapter 6, and Almehrizi 2021 analytic standard errors. Older papers remain acquisition priorities; titles, years and DOIs have been verified without treating citation lists as full-text evidence.

[Almehrizi 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jedm.12277) derives delta-method SEs for components, universe/error variances and coefficients in one- and two-facet **crossed** designs, under multivariate normality. This is not a generic nested-design or nonnormal-result guarantee. It was online 2020 and published in the 2021 issue.

[Betebenner 1998](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED423304.pdf) makes a critical algebraic point: a nonnegative variance component can have an estimator expressed as a **signed** linear combination of expected mean squares. The simple p×i person estimate subtracts the interaction mean square. An approximation derived for positive combinations of independent chi-squares does not automatically apply to that difference. His balanced all-random normal-model adaptation draws on Graybill/Wang 1980, Lu/Graybill/Burdick 1988, and Ting/Burdick/Graybill/Jeyaratnam/Lu 1990. These originals and the Burdick/Graybill 1992 monograph should be read before making a numerical oracle. Bell/Khuri simultaneous bounds are another distinct target: marginal confidence intervals do not establish familywise coverage for all components or functions.

A bibliographic correction matters here: Betebenner's reference gives Lu 1988 an erroneous page range; DOI/Crossref confirms *JSPI*18(1),35–43, not 375–380.

## Bootstrap and jackknife: conditional sampling is a design choice

The original [ACT 87-7](https://www.act.org/content/dam/act/unsecured/documents/ACT_RR87-07.pdf) compares normal/binary p×i resampling. Component-specific bootstrap plans behave differently: bootstrapping persons can miss item variation, while independently drawing individual cells destroys the crossed dependence. Its conclusions favor jackknife in studied settings; later papers sometimes find otherwise. These are differences to investigate under their actual designs, distributions and targets, not reasons to crown one method universally.

ACT 87-7 AppendixB is unusually important for finite sampling. Its pseudo-value jackknife includes corrections `1 − n/N` and `1 − k/K`; it explicitly corrects the extra square-root-of-nk divisor suggested by Cronbach 1972. Its finite-population empirical experiment samples actual trials **without replacement**, while bootstrap replicates sample the trial's rows/columns **with replacement**. Ordinary with-replacement empirical bootstrapping is not a self-evident substitute for a finite-universe sampling distribution. The catalogue retains these details so finite-facet semantics are not lost behind a generic `bootstrap=true` switch.

[CASMA 5](https://education.uiowa.edu/sites/education.uiowa.edu/files/2026-04/casma-research-report-5-archived.pdf) studies two normal-data designs; [CASMA 15](https://education.uiowa.edu/sites/education.uiowa.edu/files/2026-04/casma-research-report-15-archived.pdf) extends to normal, dichotomous and polytomous crossed/nested responses with a particular 100×20×2 arrangement. Its rules are tentative, and no bootstrap plan performed well for the SE of absolute error variance. That report explicitly lacked rigorous derivations for its ad hoc correction formulas. The final Tong/Brennan 2007 paper should be compared to the precursor instead of counting both as independent confirmation.

[CASMA 21](https://education.uiowa.edu/sites/education.uiowa.edu/files/2026-04/casma-research-report-21-archived.pdf) supplies the more general modified expected-T-term machinery for unbiased bootstrap component estimates in random-model balanced designs. This solves a point-estimator bias problem; it does not itself prove joint covariance accuracy or ratio-interval coverage. Moore 2010's doctoral expansion of these bootstrap rules is a high-priority missing full text.

[Li 2023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0288069), with Chinese 2009/2013 precursors, compares traditional, jackknife, bootstrap and MCMC methods for p×i normal/binary/polytomous/skewed responses. Its component-specific rules are supported by a 100×20 simulation arrangement, not arbitrary multifacet G/Phi designs. Its accuracy criterion allows 25%relative percentage bias: applied to nominal 0.80 coverage, that tolerance spans 0.60–1.00, too broad to equate the paper's accuracy label with strict calibration. Its [2025 correction](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0336869) only changes *form* to *from* in the title; it changes no formulas or numerical results.

Future inference interfaces should specify which facets are sampled, held fixed or conditioned upon; whether actual sampling is with or without replacement; whether whole crossed levels, nested clusters or residuals are resampled; the fitted estimator; treatment of duplicate levels; and the statistic recalculated in each replicate. A component-specific resampling rule cannot be combined naively into a ratio distribution: components and their covariance must be propagated jointly.

## Direct coefficient intervals and D-study propagation

[Jiang et al.2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9228698/) is a particularly useful warning against title-based interpretation. The title says generalizability coefficient, but its footnote states that the analyzed formulas use **absolute error**: the target is dependability/Phi. It compares four LMM methods in normal/binary/polytomous 100×20×2 crossed and nested designs, using 1000 replications and 500 bootstrap draws per replication. Its spherical-random-effects parametric bootstrap is closest to nominal 95%coverage overall, but the nested polytomous case is around 81%. Best among four candidates is still not calibrated in every scenario. The component-level follow-up also varies facet counts; no multivariate, finite-universe or arbitrary D-study guarantee is established. Public PMC HTML was acquired; inaccessible publisher/PMC PDFs were not bypassed.

[Shin and Raudenbush 2012](https://wtgrantfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/Confidence-Bounds-and-Power-for-the-Reliability-of-Observational-Measures-on-the-Quality-of-a-Social-Setting.pdf) explicitly separates uncertainty estimated in a G study from reliability/power anticipated under a proposed D study. It derives expected confidence intervals and illustrates allocation tradeoffs for a Gaussian observer/classroom/item model including balanced incomplete blocks. Its discussion records negative lower reliability bounds in some settings and considers highest-density intervals. It is strong evidence that design optimization should consider uncertainty, but its formulas must not be transplanted into arbitrary designs without checking assumptions.

## Boundaries, negative estimates and covariance constraints

ANOVA component estimators can be negative although population variances are nonnegative. A raw unbiased estimate, clipping one component after all EMS equations are solved, propagating the clipped value into other equations, and a constrained ML/REML estimate are different procedures with different sampling distributions. The library should retain raw estimates, constraints and diagnostics as explicit provenance. An interval method must match the actual estimation/constraint policy. Generic symmetric Wald intervals and generic chi-square likelihood-ratio references need additional justification at zero.

[Self and Liang 1987](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/asymptotic-properties-of-maximum-likelihood-estimators-and-likeli-4/) supplies cone/projection boundary theory; Stram/Lee 1994 develops it for longitudinal mixed effects. Some simple boundary tests have chi-square mixtures, but nuisance parameters on the boundary can invalidate that simplification. These are model-specific asymptotic results, not a turnkey rule for every crossed GT likelihood.

For multivariate GT, covariance components may have negative off-diagonals legitimately; they nevertheless require positive semidefinite matrices as population covariance objects. Elementwise nonnegative clipping is not a valid general matrix constraint. This is a mathematical design implication; this lane has not completed the multivariate estimator-specific PSD literature audit. The multivariate lane and original variance-component monographs remain the relevant follow-up.

The [Zhang/Ekvall/Molstad 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00255) is a promising current frontier: uniformly valid finite-sample intervals for Gaussian variance components and near-unity variance proportions, including nuisance boundaries and a crossed-effects example. Its dependent-data splitting requires care. No peer-reviewed version or GT-specific coefficient/D-study adaptation was verified; it is a research candidate, not an adopted algorithm.

## Simulation evidence required before implementation claims

[Morris/White/Crowther 2019](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10066118/) provides the methodological scaffold: define aims, generators, estimands, methods and performance measures; quantify Monte Carlo standard errors; preserve failed fits instead of silently conditioning on successes. A useful GT simulation grid should vary facet counts independently, crossed/nested/mixed/finite designs, tiny/zero/large components, G near zero/one, nonnormal/dichotomous/polytomous responses, missingness/imbalance where supported, and projection designs different from the fitted design.

Coverage needs its own Monte Carlo precision: `MCSE = sqrt(coverage × (1 − coverage) / repetitions)`. With 1000 replications and 0.95 coverage this is about 0.0069, so tiny differences between methods cannot be called decisive without paired comparisons and uncertainty. Bootstrap draws per dataset and independent simulation datasets solve different precision problems. A successful point-estimation bias correction does not establish correct SE, interval coverage or reliable design selection.

Atılgan 2013 and Charter 1999 are retained as primary evidence behind commonly repeated minimum-person recommendations. Their scopes differ and do not establish a universal 50/400-person threshold for multifacet estimation, nor do they resolve very few rater/task levels. Documentation should link the sample-size claim to its target and design.

## Acquisition and research gaps

Highest-value operator acquisitions: Smith 1978/1982, Bell 1986/Khuri 1981, the final Tong/Brennan 2007 pair, Brennan 2001 chapter 6, Almehrizi 2021, Gao/Brennan 2001, Wiley doctoral thesis and Moore 2010, and Burdick/Graybill 1992. Outstanding conceptual work: finite-universe resampling beyond the original p×i jackknife; conditional/unconditional uncertainty under fixed and changing facets; arbitrary nested and unbalanced coefficient intervals; singular and near zero multivariate covariance components; simultaneous uncertainty over a searched D-study design grid; and selection-induced optimism when choosing a design after estimating components. Owen's pigeonhole/product-weight results concern variance of a mean in crossed arrays; they cannot be presented as a theorem for coefficient ratios. These gaps are genuine opportunities for crolib once a literature-grounded specification and validation design are complete.
