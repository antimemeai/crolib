# Acquisition synthesis and reading route

The surveyed corpus supports a substantial generalizability-theory library.
Its mathematical branches include design algebra and sampling universes,
variance/covariance estimation, multivariate score construction, uncertainty and
measurement-design decisions. The lane reports supply sources and actual reading
evidence. This pass prepares a detailed mathematical design; it does not complete
that design or validate downloaded numerical implementations.

## Reading sequence

1. Read the acquired **Brennan ACT26 and CASMA1** for crossed/nested algebra,
   relative/absolute errors and coefficient families. Check scanned equations
   visually before deriving fixtures.
2. Acquire the **1963/1965 trilogy, Cronbach et al.1972 and Brennan2001**.
   Brennan's later chapters cover uncertainty, irregular estimation,
   multivariate designs and regressed scores.
3. Read **Allal1986 and the Cardinet sources** for symmetry, multifaceted
   objects and finite-random universes. Pair originals with their errata.
4. Read **CASMA51, The Problem of One**, before specifying estimability.
   A single prompt/rater can confound components needed for generalization;
   more observations under the same confounding do not resolve it.
5. Study **Henderson1953, Harville1977, Corbeil–Searle1976 and GT estimation
   tutorials**. Separate moment, likelihood and Bayesian contracts. Huynh's
   incomplete-data and Brennan/Huebner examples provide future comparisons.
6. Study the **matrix/composite and extended-MGT papers**, followed by profile,
   subscore and difference-score examples. Establish component linkage and
   score metrics before optimizing weights.
7. Study **ACT1987/CASMA bootstrap reports and modern interval comparisons**.
   Identify resampled facets and whether intervals concern components,
   G/Phi, projected designs or conditional scores.
8. Read the **decision, application and software reports**. Reconcile formulas
   and printed tables before treating an example as an oracle.

## Architectural implications to investigate

These are inferences from the corpus, not implementation specifications.

**Design and universe need separate declarations.** Observed rows do not define
the intended sampling universe. Crossing, nesting, finite-random/fixed facets,
multiple objects and aliasing change what components and projections mean.

**Estimator choice belongs in results.** Negative moments, clipping,
constrained boundary fits, singular covariance and failed convergence require
different interpretations. Bootstrap uncertainty cannot repair nonidentifiability.

**Multivariate scores need covariance and explicit metrics.** Weighted
composites depend on component matrices and linked facets. Means, totals, range
contributions and effective weights differ. Difference-score reliability needs
shared universe/error covariance, not just marginal reliabilities.

**Model bridges must retain their scale.** SEM, categorical latent-response,
IRT and location-scale formulations introduce explicit assumptions. Squaring a
logistic posterior standard deviation does not convert it into observed
binary-score variance.

**Decision studies have distinct targets.** Ranking, absolute dependability,
cut-score indices, classification consistency and classification accuracy answer
different questions. Continuous allocation, integer design and nested budgets
also require different optimization contracts. Small feasible enumerations can
independently check reported optima.

**Uncertainty follows the sampling model.** Finite sampling, fixed facets,
covariance constraints, zero components and near-unity coefficients have
different inferential cases. A reproducible interval construction can still have
poor empirical coverage.
